This interactive GIS map identifies the parcels, environmental features, terrain conditions, and geologic information used to define and evaluate the West Meade Environmental Overlay District.

About the West Meade Hillside Environmental Overlay District

The West Meade Hillside Environmental Overlay District is an overlay designed to establish objective development standards that protect the long-term stability and function of environmentally sensitive hillside lands to promote public health, safety, and welfare, while allowing reasonable development consistent with the natural characteristics of the site. The overlay encourages development that minimizes unnecessary grading, excavation, blasting, tree removal, and other forms of land disturbance to avoid the risk of landslides, erosion, excessive stormwater runoff, and other avoidable geologic and hydrologic hazards.

About the map

The map shows the geographic relationship between the Overlay District and the natural features it is designed to protect, including steep slopes, ridgelines, tree canopy, problem soils, documented landslide areas, elevation contours, and bedrock geologic boundaries.

Using the map

  • Use the layer control to turn individual map layers on or off.
  • Search for a parcel by address in the Search Address box.
  • Click mapped features and parcels to view available attribute information.
  • Zoom in for parcel-level detail and out to view the broader hillside system.
  • NOTE: Parcels in blue have been identified as being included in the West Meade Environmental Overlay District.

Data sources

Map layers incorporate publicly available geographic information from Metro Nashville, state and federal geologic and environmental sources, and project-specific GIS analysis prepared for the West Meade Environmental Overlay District.

Map note: This GIS map is a visual reference to the Overlay District and its supporting environmental data. Where a legal description, adopted ordinance, parcel record, or other controlling document differs from the map display, the controlling official record governs.
